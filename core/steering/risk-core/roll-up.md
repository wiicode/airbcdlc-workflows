# Roll-up — from a failing control to the executive's five rows

Residual is computed, not asserted. It rolls up the tree so that an executive reading five to seven rows is reading the true state of everything beneath them.

## The three levels

1. **Branch residual is read from control health.** A branch dispositioned Mitigate has the residual its controls actually deliver. If a linked control's implementation record shows `failing` or `degraded`, the branch is quietly back toward inherent, and the `residual-from-control-health` sensor says so. A mitigation is only as real as the control under it on the day you look.

2. **Parent residual is the aggregate of its branches.** A parent is not safer than its worst undecided or under-controlled branch. The parent's residual is computed from the branch residuals (field guide: "for a parent risk, it is the sum of residuals on the branches").

3. **Root exposure is the roll-up compared to its tolerance.** Each top-level harm's exposure is the aggregate of its parents, read against the harm's tolerance. When the roll-up exceeds the threshold, the tolerance is breached — and the accountable seat raises it within the quarter.

## The five zoom views — same tree, different altitude

| View | For | Shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **by harm** | executives | five to seven roots, exposure vs tolerance, the one metric per root |
| **by risk** | the decision forum | parents with their dispositions and residuals |
| **by branch** | the risk-assessor | branches, evidence, illustrations, control links |
| **by control** | operations | controls, implementation %, health, which branches they treat |
| **by tool** | engineering | systems, what they implement, evidence and findings hanging on them |

All five are compiled from the same records; none is hand-edited. Zooming does not change the tree, only the altitude.

## The one metric

Per root: the **share of risks with a recorded decision and a named owner**. Not a risk score — a governance-coverage number. It is the single figure the quarterly review leads with, because it answers the only question that matters at that altitude: *are we actually deciding our risks, or just holding them?*

## What this is not

Not a heat map or a composite risk score — there is no invented arithmetic that collapses likelihood and severity into a number. The roll-up aggregates residuals and compares to a stated threshold; every grade states its rule. Not a real-time dashboard pretending to be truth — it is computed from the records, which are the truth, and it carries the date it was computed.
